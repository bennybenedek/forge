# Rogue Commander Story Guide

This file defines the shared narrative context and general writing rules for Rogue Commander.

These rules apply primarily to player-facing story content, dialogue, narration, flavor text, and narrative events.
Technical documentation, structured game data, UI labels, and internal lore notes may use simpler and more functional language where appropriate.

## General Story Information

### Setting and Timeline

* Rogue Commander takes place in the Magic: The Gathering Multiverse after the canonical March of the Machine / New Phyrexian invasion.
* New Phyrexia has been displaced from the accessible Multiverse and Omenpaths now connect many planes.
* Omenpaths allow interplanar travel without requiring a Planeswalker spark, but they are not necessarily stable, predictable, or safe.
* Rogue Commander expands on this canonical state with its own events and story.
* For the chronological events before and during the game, see `story-timeline.md`.

### The Player

* The player character is referred to as the Commander.
* The Commander should remain compatible with player projection unless the story explicitly establishes otherwise.
* NPCs may address the player as "Commander."
* The Commander travels through Omenpaths, encounters Planebounds, fights battles, meets NPCs, acquires cards and resources, and repeatedly returns to the Aether between Runs.

### The Aether

* Is the central between-Run hub.
* Teferi created it as a place outside ordinary space and time and intended it to remain protected from threats such as Phyrexia.
* During the Second Phyrexian Invasion, the Aether was attacked and heavily damaged. Many of its structures, machines, and facilities were destroyed.
* The Aether still retains traces or memories of what previously existed there.
* The player gradually rebuilds the Aether over the course of the game.

### Echoes

* Are remnants of things that once existed, happened, or left a meaningful imprint.
* The Commander brings Echoes back from Runs.
* Within the damaged Aether, these Echoes can be used to restore structures and machines whose presence still remains imprinted there.
* The player therefore does not simply spend a generic meta-progression currency on passive upgrades. Progression represents the physical and functional restoration of the Aether.

### Runs and Narrative Structure

* A Run is an expedition from the Aether through connected Planes and to their leaders, the Planebounds.
* A **Trail** is the route a Run follows through a sequence of Omenpaths and Planes, ending at the Run's final Planebound.
* An **Omenpath** is an individual connection between planes; a Trail may therefore contain multiple Omenpaths.
* The random Planes, Planebounds, Elites, and Bosses encountered during a Run are not tied to specific main-story milestones.
* Main-story progression happens after completed Runs, when the Commander returns to the Aether.
* Story delivery should generally remain brief enough that gameplay can continue quickly. Rogue Commander is not structured around long mandatory conversations after every encounter.
* Major information is revealed across successive completed Runs and Descension milestones.

### Central Story Premise

* Unrelated Omenpaths are behaving abnormally, and Planebounds encountered along them have become hostile.
* Something is corrupting the Planebounds and affecting the Omenpath network.
* The nature, origin, and purpose of this threat are discovered gradually.
* The story is connected to Phyrexia and the consequences of March of the Machine, but this must not be revealed prematurely.
* Characters must only act on information they possess at the current story milestone.
* Do not use future knowledge from `story-timeline.md` in earlier scenes.

For the chronological development, reveals, previous events and ending,
see `story-timeline.md`.

### Relationship to MTG Canon

* Rogue Commander builds on Magic: The Gathering lore but also contains original events, characters, locations, technology, and developments.

* Distinguish between:
** MTG Canon -> Established Magic lore.
** Rogue Commander Canon -> Facts established specifically by this project.

* When official MTG lore and Rogue Commander additions coexist, preserve established MTG characterization and terminology unless the game's story explicitly establishes a change.

### Narrative Tone

* The story should feel like Magic: The Gathering without imitating generic high-fantasy narration.
* Prefer concrete actions, locations, objects, decisions, and character reactions over abstract statements about destiny, eternity, darkness, hope, or fate.
* Serious events may remain serious. Humor should come from characters whose personalities support it, not from a general requirement that scenes contain jokes.
* Avoid making every event sound monumental.
* Small moments are allowed to remain small.
* Characters should sound like individuals rather than different versions of the same narrative voice.

## General Constraints for writing Story or Lore

1. Start directly with the narrative action, dialogue, or relevant situation. Avoid filler openings, throat-clearing transitions, and unnecessary summary endings.
2. Vary sentence lengths naturally (incorporate fragments, short sentences, and longer multi-clause sentences). Avoid rhythmic uniformity.
3. Avoid habitual rule-of-three constructions, symmetrical paragraph structures and repeated rhetorical patterns.
4. Avoid contrastive framing ("It wasn't just X, it was Y"). Speak affirmatively and directly, and state intended ideas clearly.
5. Avoid over-polished or overly literary cliches and generic fantasy language. Words such as: delve, realm, tapestry, testament, beacon, dance, whisper, supercharge should not be used as decorative narrative vocabulary. 
Established game terminology such as Echoes is exempt.
6. Ground scenes in concrete, physical details rather than abstract or metaphorical observations.
7. Avoid aesthetic-core language such as "symphony," "mosaic," "dance," or "crescendo" unless referring to those things literally.
8. Do not use false-epiphany phrases such as "In that moment, she realized...", "Something shifted...", "Little did they know...", or "A breath they didn't know they were holding."
9. Avoid dead sensory clichés and language such as "the silence was deafening," "palpable tension," "tension hung heavy," or "eyes holding a lifetime of secrets."
10. Prefer direct description over unnecessary hedging. Avoid constructions such as "seemed to," "began to," "could," or "might" when the narrator knows what is happening. 
Preserve uncertainty when the character or narrator genuinely does not know.
11. Do not explain lore merely because the information exists. Reveal information when the current scene, character, or player needs it.
12. Prefer character-specific reactions over generic emotional narration. Show personality through what a character notices, says, avoids saying, or chooses to do.
13. Do not force every scene to end with a dramatic line, revelation, joke, or emotional conclusion. Scenes may end naturally when their purpose is complete.
