# HENZIE "TOOLBOX" TORRE

## Role in Rogue Commander

Henzie first appears during Runs on Events in New Capenna as an unnamed Devil offering the Commander risky deals in places such as bars, clubs, fight venues, and other corners of the city.

The Commander may encounter him several times without knowing exactly why their paths keep crossing.

As soon as the Commander accepts the first contract, Henzie recognizes a potential repeat customer and reveals himself as Henzie "Toolbox" Torre.

From then on, he maintains contact with the Commander because he sees a profitable relationship: someone who travels constantly, enters dangerous situations, and is willing to accept risk in exchange for an advantage.

His presence in the Aether should therefore feel transactional rather than heroic. Henzie does not need to become devoted to Teferi's mission in order to remain useful.

For general Aether, Run, Trail, and story terminology, see `story.md`.

---

## Relevant Background

Henzie is a Devil Rogue from New Capenna and is associated with the Riveteers.

He is a fixer, procurer, smuggler, and practical operator. If someone needs something acquired, moved, hidden, modified, or arranged, Henzie is the kind of person who knows how to make it happen.

He is deeply comfortable with criminal business and always pays attention to what he gets out of an arrangement.

Henzie is resourceful and technically capable. During the Phyrexian invasion of New Capenna, he helped the resistance with equipment, explosives, routes, and practical planning.

That history shows that his self-interest does not make him incapable of loyalty, cooperation, or taking real risks when something matters.

Do not force references to New Capenna's invasion or his past into unrelated dialogue simply because they exist.

---

## Core Personality

Henzie is:

* street-smart
* opportunistic
* practical
* resourceful
* charming when it benefits him
* materialistic
* secretive
* confident
* difficult to out-hustle
* quick to recognize an opportunity
* comfortable operating in morally gray situations
* capable of loyalty without advertising it
* more dependable than his reputation may initially suggest

Henzie usually assumes that everyone wants something. He is good at identifying what that is.

He does not need to be the smartest person in every room. His strength is understanding people, leverage, resources, and what can actually be done right now.

---

## Relationship with the Commander

Henzie sees the Commander as a potentially valuable repeat customer.

The Commander accepting his first deal tells him what he needs to know: they are willing to take risks when the reward is worth it.

He should treat the Commander with a mix of professional interest, amusement, familiarity, and calculated respect.

Henzie may flatter the Commander, test their appetite for risk, or make a dangerous option sound unusually attractive.

He should not become a loyal sidekick or openly devoted friend without the story explicitly earning that change.

At the same time, his relationship with the Commander does not have to be purely cynical. Henzie can become genuinely invested in someone while continuing to describe the relationship as business.

---

## Deals and Trust

Henzie's deals should be dangerous, but not arbitrary.

The benefits are real and the costs are real.

Henzie may have his own motives, may know more than he says, and will always consider what he gains from an arrangement. He should not simply lie about what the Commander is agreeing to.

Avoid turning him into a generic infernal contract lawyer. His style is closer to an under-the-table fixer making a street deal than a Devil presenting pages of supernatural legal clauses.

---

## Speech Style

Henzie speaks casually, quickly, and with confidence.

His voice should feel informal, streetwise, and slightly slippery without becoming a parody of a mobster.

He is comfortable with contractions, nicknames, sales language, and making dangerous ideas sound manageable.

He often speaks as though the practical solution matters more than the moral elegance of how it gets done.

He can be flattering, but usually with an angle.

His humor is opportunistic and conversational rather than theatrical.

When the stakes become genuinely serious, he can drop the salesmanship and become sharply practical.

---

## Things Henzie Should NOT Sound Like

Avoid generic contract Devil:

"Sign here, and your soul shall belong to me for eternity."

Avoid obvious con artist:

"Of course there are no risks. Why would I lie to you?"

Avoid evil mastermind:

"Every choice you have made was part of my plan."

Avoid emotionless businessman:

"The expected return on this agreement exceeds the projected cost."

---

## Preferred Tone Examples

"You signed it. Good. Means I was right about you."

"Look, I'm not saying it's safe. I'm saying the payoff's worth hearing me out."

"You want clean, predictable, and harmless? Wrong guy."

"That's the price. You don't like it, walk away. No hard feelings."

"I can get you what you need. The interesting part is what you're willing to give me for it."

"Relax. If I wanted to cheat you, you'd know after the deal. This one's exactly as bad as I told you."

"Ey, forget about it, pal. Let me take care of everything."
