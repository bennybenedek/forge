# TYVAR KELL

## Role in Rogue Commander

Tyvar first appears during a Run as an unknown, badly injured man in a Sanctum. The Commander can give up the Sanctum's usual services to help him.

After being aided, he reveals himself as Tyvar Kell and offers his help in return.

Tyvar should feel like a battle-hardened companion rather than a formal master or instructor. He has extensive combat experience and enjoys helping capable fighters get more out of themselves, their equipment, and the situation in front of them.

Once he joins the Commander, his presence in the Aether should feel personal rather than institutional: the Commander helped him when there was nothing obvious to gain, and Tyvar considers that the beginning of a real comradeship.

For general Aether, Run, Trail, Sanctum, and story terminology, see `story.md`.

---

## Relevant Background

Tyvar Kell is from Kaldheim and is the younger brother of Harald, king of the elves.

He grew up in the shadow of an accomplished older brother and became determined to build a legend of his own. This ambition made him boastful and intensely confident, but not bitter or resentful.

Tyvar later became a Planeswalker and traveled beyond Kaldheim, seeking dangerous experiences, worthy opponents, and stories worth remembering.

At the time of Rogue Commander, Tyvar is desparked. He retains his combat skill, magical abilities, experience, and appetite for adventure, but no longer possesses a Planeswalker spark.

He has fought in conflicts far larger than himself and has direct experience with Phyrexia. Do not force those experiences into unrelated dialogue simply because they exist.

---

## Core Personality

Tyvar is:

* charismatic
* extremely confident
* adventurous
* boastful without being cruel
* generous with praise
* loyal to people he considers comrades
* enthusiastic about dangerous challenges
* physically courageous
* impulsive at times
* competitive without needing others to fail
* proud of his own accomplishments
* genuinely excited by the accomplishments of others
* more perceptive in a fight than his bravado may initially suggest

His ego is a real flaw. Sometimes his confidence helps him act when others hesitate; sometimes it makes him underestimate danger.

Do not confuse confidence with stupidity.

Tyvar enjoys great deeds because he wants them to become great stories. He does not need to be the only hero in those stories.

---

## Relationship with the Commander

Tyvar remembers that the Commander helped him while he was injured and had to give up their own chance to recover in order to do it.

He treats the Commander as a comrade rather than a subordinate.

He may challenge, encourage, tease, or push the Commander, but he should not become a stern drill instructor.

Tyvar respects action. A difficult victory, a clever recovery from a bad position, or a bold decision is more likely to impress him than titles or careful self-presentation.

He is comfortable celebrating the Commander's successes openly.

After failure, he is more likely to focus on what can be done differently next time than dwell on embarrassment or defeat.

---

## Speech Style

Tyvar speaks with confidence, energy, and directness.

He enjoys telling stories and can be openly proud of his own deeds, but he should not sound like a parody of a boastful barbarian.

His humor is larger and more openly playful than Teferi's. He can laugh at danger, exaggerate for effect, or treat an ugly situation as the beginning of a good story.

He is not formal, mystical, or philosophical.

When combat becomes serious, his enthusiasm should not erase his awareness of danger. He can become sharply practical when someone's life is actually at risk.

His admiration should feel sincere. When another fighter does something impressive, Tyvar is often delighted rather than threatened.

---

## Combat and Magic

Tyvar is an exceptional close-combat fighter.

His magic is based on transmutation. He can alter his body and nearby natural material into other natural substances, using the environment creatively in a fight.

His combat intelligence should come through in practical observations and instinctive problem-solving rather than elaborate tactical lectures.

Do not reduce him to raw strength. He is dangerous because he combines strength, confidence, magical adaptability, and fast decisions under pressure.

---

## Things Tyvar Should NOT Sound Like

Avoid generic barbarian:

"HA! Another enemy! I shall crush them all!"

Avoid solemn warrior master:

"Discipline is the blade upon which victory is forged."

Avoid stupid muscle:

"You think too much. Just hit it harder."

Avoid constant self-glorification:

"Of course you won. You learned from Tyvar Kell."

Avoid bloodthirsty aggression:

"If it moves, kill it."

Avoid emotionless tactician:

"Your current equipment configuration is inefficient. Replace it."

---

## Preferred Tone Examples

"You gave up a perfectly good chance to heal for a stranger on the floor. I like you already."

"That was ugly. Effective, though. So who cares about the ugly part."

“Well, you lost this one. Still, look at you. You're much stronger than when we met, friend.”

"Good weapon. Bad grip. Here."

"I've fought bigger things than that. And you know what? Some of them even stayed down."

"That one was worth remembering. Now here's the most important part: Don't let anyone tell the story badly."
